SENIOR AI PLANS - SECTION 2 (INS-01 to INS-40) COMPLIANCE REVIEW

Reviewed 2026-11-25. Compliance guidance, not a legal opinion.
Basis: CY2027 MA/Part D marketing rule (applies to marketing from Oct 1, 2026).

ITEMS TO FIX
------------
INS-01  TPMO disclaimer
  - The current text no longer mentions SHIP. Use:
    "We do not offer every plan available in your area. Currently we represent [#]
    organizations which offer [#] products in your area. Please contact Medicare.gov
    or 1-800-MEDICARE to get information on all of your options."
  - On calls, say it before any benefits are discussed (no longer "within the first minute").
  - Source: 42 CFR 422.2267
    https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-V/section-422.2267

INS-06  48-hour SOA wait
  - Outdated. CMS dropped the 48-hour wait. The appointment can happen any time after
    the SOA is done, and SOAs can now be collected at educational events.
  - Still required: an SOA before the appointment, in writing if in person, valid 12 months.
  - Take the 48-hour minimum off the Cal.com booking types.
  - Source: 42 CFR 422.2264
    https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-V/section-422.2264

INS-04 and INS-34  Call recordings
  - CMS now requires 6 years, not 10. Years 1-3 must be audio; years 4-6 can be audio
    or full transcripts. Keeping them 10 years is allowed; state 6 as the legal minimum.
  - The rest of INS-04 is right: Florida is all-party consent, so announce recording on
    every call.

INS-13  Sharing leads
  - "Never share" is fine. If leads are ever shared, CMS requires prior written consent
    that names each receiving company and lets the person accept or reject each one
    separately. Applies to manually dialed calls too.
  - A live transfer with spoken consent on the call is allowed. Sharing with the carrier
    itself does not need this consent.
  - Source: 42 CFR 422.2274
    https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-V/section-422.2274

INS-15  Superlatives
  - Proof no longer has to appear on the material itself, but the data must be on file
    if CMS asks. Citing the source on the page is still fine and is the safer choice.

ITEMS THAT HOLD UP
------------------
INS-02, 03, 05, 07-12, 14, 16-33, 35-40.
Note on INS-10/11: the FCC one-to-one consent rule was struck down in 2025 (IMC v. FCC),
but the consent text does not rely on it. Prior express written consent still covers
autodialed and prerecorded calls and texts.

CALIFORNIA ADDENDUM
=====================================================================================
Added 2026-11-25. Compliance
guidance, not a legal opinion. Federal CMS rules (the sections above) still control
Medicare Advantage and Part D marketing. In general, California law does not regulate MA
or Part D plans themselves, but it does regulate the agent's license, Medigap, the
ancillary products (dental/vision/hearing and hospital indemnity, which California treats
as "disability insurance"), final expense life insurance, privacy, and call recording.
Statute links go to leginfo.legislature.ca.gov unless noted.

INS-01  TPMO disclaimer
  - No change for MA/Part D. Medigap advertising has a separate California disclaimer. Every
    Medigap ad must prominently show, in substance: "Not connected with or endorsed by the
    U.S. Government or the federal Medicare program." Ins. Code 10192.19(h).
    https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/insurance-code/ins-sect-10192-19/
  - Medigap ads that are not a full sales presentation must also say, in 10-point or larger
    UPPERCASE type, that an outline of coverage is available on request. If someone asks,
    the agent must send it within 14 days. Ins. Code 10192.17(l).
    https://california.public.law/codes/insurance_code_section_10192.17

INS-03  No government look-alikes
  - Holds, and California puts it in statute. Ins. Code 787(c)-(e) (disability and life ads
    aimed at people 65+) and 10192.19(e)-(g) (Medigap) ban names, symbols, or wording that
    suggest a government, Social Security, or Medicare connection. Ads also may not suggest
    the reader will lose a benefit by not responding (787(f), 10192.19(i)).
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=INS&sectionNum=787

INS-04  Call recording
  - California is an all-party consent state. Penal Code 632 makes it a crime to record a
    confidential communication without the consent of all parties, and Penal Code 632.7
    covers cell and cordless calls. Penal Code 637.2 allows private lawsuits for $5,000 per
    violation.
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=632
  - The file's approach (announce recording at the start of every call, "by continuing you
    agree") is right for California. Give the notice before any substantive talk, and say it
    again if a new person joins or the call is transferred.
  - Also covers website chat and AI tools. Plaintiffs sue under Penal Code 631 (wiretapping)
    over chat widgets, session replay, and pixels when a vendor captures what visitors type.
    SB 690 (signed Sept 30, 2026, operative Jan 1, 2027) only ends private suits under
    Penal Code 638.51 (pen register) for website conduct. Section 631 suits are unaffected.
    Keep chat click-to-load, show a recording/vendor notice before the first message, and use
    vendors that are contractually barred from using chat data for their own purposes.
    https://www.mccarter.com/insights/california-finally-addressed-cipa-website-claims-just-not-all-of-them/

INS-05 / INS-06  SOA and booking
  - No extra California SOA rule for MA/Part D. The CMS SOA rules (no 48-hour wait) control.
  - NEW for in-home final expense (life insurance) or annuity meetings with anyone 65+:
    Ins. Code 789.10 requires a separate written notice delivered at least 24 hours and no
    more than 14 days before the first meeting in the senior's home. If an existing client
    asks for a same-day visit, the notice can be given at the start of the meeting.
      - Format: 16-point bold, stand-alone, containing only the listed items: agent's name
        and California license number, mailing address and phone number, the products to be
        discussed, the right to have others present, the right to end the meeting, CDI
        consumer phone numbers, and who is attending.
      - At the door, before anything except a greeting, state that the visit is about
        insurance. Leave immediately when asked.
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=INS&sectionNum=789.10

INS-07  SOA wording
  - No California change.

INS-08 / INS-09  Cross-selling
  - The CMS separation of non-health products still applies. In California, final expense
    also triggers Ins. Code 789.10 (above) and the senior advertising rules in 787. Ins. Code
    785 gives everyone 65+ a statutory duty of honesty, good faith, and fair dealing, but by
    its own terms the senior article does not apply to Medigap (785(c)(1)) unless a section
    says otherwise.
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=INS&sectionNum=785
  - Keep final expense in its own flow, as the file says.

INS-10 / INS-11  Consent for automated calls and texts
  - Telemarketing registration: insurance licensees are not "telephonic sellers" when the
    transaction is governed by the Insurance Code, so registering with the California AG is
    generally not required. Bus. & Prof. Code 17511.1(e)(4).
    https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_17511.1
  - Prerecorded and AI-voice calls: under Pub. Util. Code 2874 (amended by AB 2905, effective
    Jan 1, 2025), an automatic dialing-announcing device may only be used after a live,
    natural-voice announcement that:
      - states the nature of the call and the business's name, address, and phone number;
      - asks whether the person consents to hear the recorded message; and
      - says whether the message uses an AI-generated voice.
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PUC&sectionNum=2874
  - Practical fix: do not send prerecorded or AI-voice calls to California numbers, even with
    TCPA consent, unless counsel confirms an exemption applies. Keep the consent box optional
    (INS-11 holds).
  - Leads from ads must say an agent may contact the person. When following up a lead, the
    agent must disclose in the first contact that the name came from a lead source.
    Ins. Code 787 (intro paragraph) and 10192.19(b) (Medigap).

INS-12  Consent records
  - No California change. Keeping them 10 years is fine.

INS-13  Lead sharing
  - California adds a second gate: the Insurance Information and Privacy Protection Act
    (IIPPA), which covers agents.
      - Ins. Code 791.13 bars disclosing personal information gathered in an insurance
        transaction without the person's written authorization (dated, signed, under one year
        old if the recipient is not an insurer or agent), except for listed purposes.
      - Disclosure for marketing is allowed only without medical-record information and only
        after the person has been given an opt-out (791.13(k)).
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=INS&sectionNum=791.13
  - "Never share" (answer A) remains the right policy.

INS-14  Who approves product pages
  - California resolves the "?". Every ad used by an agent for an insurer's policy needs the
    insurer's written approval before use: Ins. Code 787(i) (disability and life ads aimed
    at people 65+) and 10192.19(l) (Medigap).
  - Medigap insurers must also file every Medigap ad with the Commissioner at least 30 days
    before use (10192.19(a)). Any California Medigap page or ad therefore goes through the
    carrier, which files it with CDI.
  - Generic education naming no carrier or policy is lower risk, but treat any Medigap,
    dental/vision/hearing, hospital indemnity, or final expense page aimed at California
    seniors as an ad.
  - New answer for CA: Yes, carrier approval is required.

INS-15  Superlatives
  - No specific California statute was found beyond the general ban on misleading ads (Ins.
    Code 790.03(b), not re-verified this pass). Keep the CMS-based fix.

INS-16 / INS-17  MA premium and out-of-pocket statements
  - No California change (federal MA rules).

INS-18  Medigap enrollment rights
  - California's birthday rule (Ins. Code 10192.11(h)): each year, an annual open
    enrollment of 60 days or more starting on the person's birthday.
      - Anyone already in a Medigap policy can buy any Medigap policy, from any carrier, with
        equal or lesser benefits, with no health questions and no higher price for health.
      - Insurers must send notice 30-60 days before the window.
      - Example: someone with Plan G can move to another carrier's Plan G or Plan N, but not
        up to Plan F.
  - Other California guaranteed-issue rights in 10192.11:
      - (d): 6 months for people under 65 on Medicare due to disability, starting from Part B
        enrollment.
      - (e): 6 months after losing employer or retiree coverage (including COBRA/Cal-COBRA)
        or military base coverage.
      - (f): moving out of the plan's area.
      - (g): an extra 60 days added onto the federal window when an MA plan terminates.
      - (i): losing Medi-Cal, or having share-of-cost Medi-Cal that hasn't been met.
  - Under 65: issuers must offer plans A, B, D, G, plus K or L and M or N, to people newly
    eligible since 2020. The statute text still excludes people with ESRD.
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=INS&sectionNum=10192.11
    NAIC chart (May 11, 2026):
    https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/inline-files/medigapbdayruleandunder65chart.pdf
  - SB 1236 (2024) proposed a 90-day guaranteed-issue window every Jan 1. The current
    statute text and the May 2026 NAIC chart do not show it, so treat it as NOT enacted.
    Re-verify before publishing.

INS-19  "Several companies" claims
  - No California change.

INS-20  "Free" claims and gifts
  - No new California limit found. CMS nominal-value limits apply.

INS-21  "Same premium" claim
  - Holds. In California an appointed agent may not charge any fee the insurer has not
    authorized (CDI Bulletin 80-6), so for Medigap the premium really is the same.
    https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0250-insurers/0300-insurers/0200-bulletins/bulletin-notices-commiss-opinion/upload/Bulletin80-6.pdf

INS-22  Ancillary product notices
  - Hospital indemnity and other disability policies sold to people eligible for Medicare
    must carry this notice on the outline of coverage or policy (insurer duty, 12-point
    type): "THIS [POLICY] IS NOT A MEDICARE SUPPLEMENT [POLICY]. If you are eligible for
    Medicare, review the Guide to Health Insurance for People with Medicare available from
    the company." Ins. Code 10192.17(k).
  - The application must disclose how much the policy duplicates Medicare, and the Guide to
    Health Insurance for People with Medicare must be delivered (10192.17).
  - Mirror this wording on California product pages and use the carrier's state forms.
  - Event ads: Ins. Code 787(k) bars calling a sales event a "seminar," "class,"
    "informational meeting," "benefits assistance," or similar unless "and insurance sales
    presentation" follows immediately, in the same font and type size (disability and life
    products aimed at 65+).

INS-23  HSA and Part A timing
  - No California change.

INS-24  License display
  - Required in California (Ins. Code 1725.5, amended by AB 1578, effective 2024):
      - The CDI license number (not the NPN) must appear on business cards, written price
        quotes, and print ads distributed only in California. Size: at least as large as any
        phone number, address, or fax number shown, or 12-point (8-point on business cards),
        whichever is larger.
      - Print ads and quotes must also include the word "Insurance."
      - The license number must be in every email involving licensed activity, right next to
        or on the line below the sender's name (or the organization's name).
      - Fines: $200, then $500, then $1,000.
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=INS&sectionNum=1725.5
  - Websites are not named in the statute. Showing the CA license number in the footer and
    on every quote/PDF is the safe default.
  - Add the license number to the agents' email signatures and the Resend templates.

INS-25 / INS-26  Screen-outs and retiree coverage
  - Add "Medi-Cal" wording for California duals, and note the Medi-Cal-loss Medigap right
    (10192.11(i)).

INS-27 / INS-28 / INS-29  AI plan match
  - Bot disclosure: Bus. & Prof. Code 17941 makes it unlawful to use a bot to communicate with
    a person in California while misleading them about its artificial identity in order to
    encourage a sale. A clear, conspicuous "you're chatting with an automated assistant"
    disclosure gives safe harbor.
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17941
  - CCPA automated decisionmaking (ADMT) rules: approved Sept 22, 2025. Duties for using
    ADMT to make "significant decisions" (which include healthcare services) start Jan 1,
    2027: pre-use notice, opt-out, and access rights.
      - They apply only if the agency is a CCPA "business" (see INS-36).
      - A tool whose output a licensed agent reviews and can change does not "substantially
        replace human decisionmaking."
      - The file's approach (education only, a licensed agent makes any recommendation)
        keeps the tool outside ADMT. Document that human review.
    https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/ccpa_updates.html
  - CDI Bulletin 2022-5 tells all licensees to avoid unfair discrimination, including
    through algorithms and outside data used in marketing. Don't route or rank people
    using proxies like ZIP code in ways that could disadvantage protected groups.
    https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0250-insurers/0300-insurers/0200-bulletins/bulletin-notices-commiss-opinion/upload/BULLETIN-2022-5-Allegations-of-Racial-Bias-and-Unfair-Discrimination-in-Marketing-Rating-Underwriting-and-Claims-Practices-by-the-Insurance-Industry.pdf

INS-30  Fees on top of commissions  (STRICTER in California)
  - CDI Bulletin 80-6: an agent may not charge fees the insurer has not authorized.
    Insurer-authorized fees count as premium and must not be unfairly discriminatory.
  - The "broker fee" route doesn't exist for health. Ins. Code 1623 defines a broker as
    someone transacting insurance other than life, disability, or health.
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=INS&sectionNum=1623.
  - The only fee path is a separate Life and Disability Insurance Analyst license (Ins. Code
    Chapter 8, from 1831). Even then, Ins. Code 1848 forbids fees for services "customarily
    associated with" selling, or servicing policies the licensee wrote or earns commission
    on. Any fee needs a signed written agreement stating that the same information and
    services are available from the insurer at no cost, and that the licensee earns
    commissions. Keep agreements 3 years.
    https://california.public.law/codes/insurance_code_section_1848
  - Result for CA: do not offer the Plus/Family paid tiers (priority service, plan
    monitoring, annual check) to California Medicare clients. Answer D (keep hidden) is
    correct and should be permanent for CA unless counsel clears an analyst-license model.

INS-31  Paid feature naming
  - Moot for California if the paid tiers are not offered there.

INS-32 / INS-33 / INS-34  Retention and form descriptions
  - No California minimum longer than CMS was found for SOAs, calls, or consents. An analyst fee
    agreement (if ever used) must be kept 3 years (Ins. Code 1848).

INS-35  HIPAA and health data
  - CMIA (Civ. Code 56 et seq.) mainly binds providers, health plans, and their contractors.
    Civ. Code 56.06(b) also covers a business offering consumer software "designed to
    maintain medical information" so a person can manage it or a condition.
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=56.06
  - A one-time plan-match or drug-list intake probably is not that. A saved drug/doctor
    profile that clients log in to manage could be argued to fall under it. Have counsel
    decide before adding saved profiles.
  - IIPPA (Ins. Code 791.02(q)) separately treats health details given to an agent in an
    insurance transaction as "medical record information," with the 791.13 disclosure limits.

INS-36  Privacy notices
  - CCPA/CPRA covers a for-profit business doing business in California that meets any of:
      - over $26,625,000 annual gross revenue (2025-26 inflation-adjusted figure);
      - buys, sells, or "shares" personal information of 100,000 or more California
        consumers or households a year;
      - earns 50% or more of revenue from selling or sharing personal information.
    https://privacy.ca.gov/laws-and-regulations/monetary-thresholds-in-the-ccpa/
  - A small agency likely falls below these at launch. Ad pixels count as "sharing" and can
    push it over the threshold over time, so re-check yearly.
  - Data covered by GLBA and medical information covered by CMIA/HIPAA are partly exempt
    (Civ. Code 1798.145). The 2025 CPPA regulations add insurance-specific clarifications.
  - IIPPA applies now, whatever the agency's size. Agents must give a written Notice of
    Information Practices to applicants by policy delivery (Ins. Code 791.04; an abbreviated
    notice is allowed). Access and correction rights also apply (791.08, 791.09).
    https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=INS&sectionNum=791.04
  - Add a California IIPPA notice to the client paperwork. Most carriers supply one at
    policy delivery, and an agent may rely on a carrier's notice made on its behalf
    (791.04(d)). Confirm with each carrier.

INS-37 / INS-38  Counsel and monitoring
  - Counsel should be familiar with California insurance and CIPA issues.

INS-39  Cookies and pixels
  - Holds for California, and for a stronger reason: CIPA 631 lawsuits over pixels and chat
    widgets (see INS-04).
  - If ad pixels are ever added, California needs click-to-load or prior consent. Once over
    the CCPA threshold, it also needs a "Do Not Sell or Share" link and must honor the Global
    Privacy Control browser signal.

INS-40  Change approval
  - Keep it. For California Medigap, dental/vision/hearing, hospital indemnity, and final
    expense pages, the log should also record the carrier's written approval (INS-14).
