California rule: a Medigap, dental/vision/hearing, hospital indemnity or final expense page aimed at
California seniors counts as an advertisement. It needs the **issuing carrier's written approval
before use** (Ins. Code 787(i) and 10192.19(l)). Medigap carriers also file the ad with the California
Department of Insurance at least 30 days before use (10192.19(a)). Record the approval below before
the page goes live in California. Generic education that names no carrier or policy is lower risk,
but still log it.

## Operational items the website cannot enforce

These come from the 2026-11-25 review. They are the agents' and the agency's job, not the code's.

- Announce recording at the start of every call, before anything substantive. Repeat it when someone
  joins or the call is transferred (all-party consent in California and Florida). Keep recordings at
  least 6 years: audio for years 1-3, then audio or full transcripts.
- Give the TPMO disclaimer on calls before discussing any benefits.
- Complete the Scope of Appointment before the appointment (in writing if in person). An SOA is valid
  for 12 months. There is no 48-hour wait, so remove any 48-hour minimum notice from the Cal.com
  event types.
- Don't send prerecorded or AI-voice calls to California numbers (Pub. Util. Code 2874) unless
  counsel confirms an exemption. Don't text or message anyone who hasn't asked to be contacted.
- When following up a lead, say in the first contact that the name came from a lead source (Ins. Code 787).
- In-home final expense visits with anyone 65+ in California need the 789.10 written notice delivered
  24 hours to 14 days ahead. At the door, say the visit is about insurance, and leave when asked.
- Give the California IIPPA Notice of Information Practices by policy delivery (the carrier's notice
  may cover it; confirm with each carrier).
- Never share leads with another TPMO, agency or call center (policy: never share).
- Re-check the CCPA thresholds once a year, especially if ad pixels are ever added.
